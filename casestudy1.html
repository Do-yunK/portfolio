Benny's Hub

Accessible AAC for adults with progressive disabilities

Project Description

Benny's Hub is an AAC interface redesign developed around Benny's communication needs and associated with the Narbe Foundation. I led the design and development of my version from concept through final presentation, exploring accessible, efficient interactions for adults with progressive disabilities. The project was demonstrated with an Arduino Uno and physical head-switch setup and showcased at YES Show 2026.

Problem Statement

Traditional communication interfaces can become difficult to navigate as physical abilities change. Small interaction targets, complex navigation, and unnecessary steps can add barriers.

How might we create an AAC experience that adapts to changing physical abilities while keeping communication simple and familiar?

User Research

Designing for AAC means considering communication needs, interaction methods, and how those needs may change over time. This project focuses on familiar, adaptable interactions without assuming one input method works for everyone.

01 — Navigation Barriers

Conventional touch interactions may not work for every person or situation.

Design opportunity: Explore alternative ways to navigate and select, including scanning and head-switch interaction.

02 — Interaction Load

Finding frequently used phrases across categories can require unnecessary effort.

Design opportunity: Make phrase categories and common communication tools easier to access.

03 — Changing Needs

Progressive disabilities can change how a person interacts with technology over time.

Design opportunity: Preserve a familiar experience while exploring adaptable input.

User Journey & Usability

Navigate phrase categories, scan through options, and select a phrase. Frequently used phrases and communication tools provide direct access to key interactions. Clear visual feedback, predictable navigation, consistency, and fewer unnecessary steps help keep communication straightforward.

Hero Statement

Communication should adapt to the person, not the other way around.

The interface explores different ways to interact while supporting familiarity and independence.

Information Architecture

The phrase board organizes communication into accessible categories, with routes to frequently used phrases and other communication tools. A familiar structure helps people find and select the messages they want to share.

Interface Design

Bringing It Together

The redesign explores accessible navigation, scanning, and visual selection feedback alongside a physical head-switch interaction. An Arduino Uno connected to a head-switch setup demonstrated how interaction could extend beyond a conventional touchscreen; this prototype does not imply every alternative input method is implemented.

Conclusion

Benny's Hub reinforced that AAC should support autonomy and expression as interaction needs change. Accessible communication means preserving familiarity and usability while making room for different ways to interact.
